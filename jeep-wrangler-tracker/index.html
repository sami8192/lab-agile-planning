Recurring problems to inspect for on any JK

  • Death wobble: usually a worn track bar, ball joints or steering stabilizer. Test at 45–60 mph over a bump; check for lift kits.
  • TIPM / BCM electrical faults (2007–2018): random stalling, no-start, fuel pump relay. Ask whether it has been replaced.
  • 3.6L Pentastar (2012+): listen for ticking or knocking when warm (lifter/rocker needle bearing, left head failure on early cars). Verify the oil-cooler and power-steering-line recalls were done on 2012–13 automatics.
  • Open airbag recalls: Takata inflator and clockspring recalls covered 2007–2016. Confirm all are completed by VIN, because the fix is free.
  • Hardtop leaks, soft-top condition, and underbody rust (less of an issue on California cars).
  • TJ (1997–2006): frame rust is the deal-breaker. Inspect the inside of the frame with a scope or tap test, plus the tub, fenders and tailgate.

Per-listing steps

  1. Get the VIN, then run NHTSA recalls and NICB VINCheck (free), then CarFax / AutoCheck.
  2. Owner history: 1–2 owners ideal. Check whether it was a rental, fleet, or commercial vehicle. Check the registration states (a CA car avoids salt, while an ex-Northeast car may have rust).
  3. Service history: look for regular oil changes (every ≤ 7.5k mi), transmission/transfer-case/axle fluid service, and the recall and TIPM work above. Ask for receipts.
  4. Title: must be clean. Walk away from salvage, rebuilt, flood or lemon-law buyback. A price far below market (for example, a 2015–2017 under about $11k) is a red flag until explained.
  5. Accidents: frame or structural damage is a hard no. Airbag deployment is a hard no.
  6. Modifications: stock or lightly modified is best. Heavy lift or off-road abuse raises wear on the steering, axles and driveline.
  7. Pre-purchase inspection by an independent shop (or Jeep dealer) before paying. For out-of-state cars, book one in the seller's city, as it is cheap insurance against a $1,500+ surprise.
  8. Out-of-state purchase: Colorado needs a VIN verification and title transfer, and the Denver metro area requires emissions testing on older vehicles. Confirm the current rules with the Colorado DMV before buying, and get a written bill of sale, lien release and all keys. Budget for transport (roughly $900–$1,400 from California) or a one-way trip.
